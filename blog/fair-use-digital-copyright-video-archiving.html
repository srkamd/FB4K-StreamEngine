As content creators, educators, and media archivists, navigating digital copyright law can feel like walking through a minefield. Downloading media from public websites is frequently misunderstood—some assume any download is illegal piracy, while others believe that putting "No Copyright Intended" in a caption grants immunity. Both extremes are wrong.

The Four Factors of Fair Use (17 U.S. Code § 107)

Under United States copyright law and equivalent global provisions, downloading and utilizing third-party copyrighted video is legally evaluated across four statutory balancing factors:

  1. Purpose and Character of Use: Is your use transformative? Does it add new commentary, critical analysis, or historical context rather than simply serving as a duplicate? Non-profit educational uses receive favorable weight.
  2. Nature of the Copyrighted Work: Factual, historical, or news clips receive broader fair-use leeway than fictional, theatrical entertainment works.
  3. Amount and Substantiality: You should only archive and reference the precise snippet necessary to convey your analytical point, not the full entire work.
  4. Effect Upon Potential Market: If your download and subsequent commentary replaces the market need for the original broadcast, fair use is weakened.
Legitimate Archiving Uses: Personal data backups of your own uploaded videos, academic research libraries, non-commercial commentary reels, and digital litigation evidence preservation represent well-established legal archiving practices. Always read our Terms of Service and DMCA Policy.
