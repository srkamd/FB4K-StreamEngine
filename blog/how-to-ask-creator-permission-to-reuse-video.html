How to Ask a Creator for Permission to Reuse Their Video

Copy-paste message templates and professional etiquette for requesting legal permission to repost or adapt Facebook videos.

By Shahrukh Ahmad (SRK AMD)  ·  Updated October 11, 2026  ·  6 min read

The Best Way to Ask: Clear, Polite, and Specific

The most effective way to request permission to reuse a video is to send a direct, transparent message explaining exactly who you are, which specific clip you wish to use, where you plan to post it, and how you will credit them. Content creators receive dozens of vague requests every week; a specific, professional proposal dramatically increases your chance of receiving a positive reply.

Copy-Paste Message Template

Subject: Permission request: Using your [Topic] clip in my [Project Type]

Hi [Creator Name],

I loved your recent video discussing [Brief Topic] published on [Date / Link].

I am currently producing a non-commercial educational video / podcast / article about [Your Topic], and I would love to include a [X]-second segment of your footage.

Here is how I will credit you:

  • On-screen graphic with your handle: @[Handle]
  • Direct link to your original video at the top of the description box
  • Clear vocal attribution during the clip segment

Please let me know if you are open to this, or if you have specific attribution guidelines you'd like me to follow. Thank you so much for your great content!

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Your Website or Channel Link]

Dos and Don'ts Checklist

Do This ✓ Avoid This ✗
Be specific about timestamps (e.g. 0:14 to 0:42) Don't say "Can I use your videos?" without context
Provide a link to your channel or portfolio Don't use anonymous or blank burner accounts
Offer clear attribution and prominent links Don't bury their credit in tiny unreadable text
Wait for explicit written confirmation before publishing Don't assume silence or lack of reply means "yes"

What to Do If They Decline or Don't Respond

Respect their decision. Creators own their work and are under no obligation to grant licenses. If they decline, thank them politely. If they do not respond within 7 days, look for alternative footage from Creative Commons libraries or film your own demonstration.

Negotiating Licensing & Commercial Terms

While casual educational or personal reposting often requires only clear attribution, using a video for a commercial venture (such as a brand campaign, monetized YouTube video, or paid course) requires a formal written license.

When approaching a creator for commercial rights:

  • Offer Compensation: Acknowledge their effort by proposing a licensing fee (e.g. $50 to $500 depending on reach). Creators are far more responsive when their time is valued.
  • Specify the Scope: Clarify whether you need non-exclusive rights (the creator can still license it to others) and the duration of use (e.g. 1 year or in perpetuity).
  • Draft a Simple Agreement: Send a 1-page licensing memorandum signed by both parties to avoid future royalty disputes.

Frequently Asked Questions and Practical Advice

Why is direct stream verification critical before local storage?

Direct verification ensures that the downloaded file contains complete video and audio tracks without corrupt moov atoms. On web servers, the moov atom contains the index of frames. If a stream disconnects prematurely, the resulting file will fail to seek or play properly.

How does bandwidth throttling affect mobile video downloads?

Cellular connections often throttle long-lived socket connections to prioritize real-time interactive traffic. To prevent download timeouts, utilize reliable Wi-Fi networks and avoid background app sleep modes when saving large video files.

What are the key ethical principles when handling third-party media?

Always obtain explicit written authorization before re-distributing, editing, or repurposing digital media. Archiving for offline study or personal reference must strictly align with statutory Fair Use principles and platform terms.
