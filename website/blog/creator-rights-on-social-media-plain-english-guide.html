The Foundational Law: You Own What You Create

Under international copyright law (including the Berne Convention), you automatically own the exclusive copyright to your original videos, photos, and music the second you record them to digital media. You do not need to register with a government office or include a copyright symbol (©) to have legal protection. Uploading your video to Facebook does NOT transfer your ownership to Meta.

What Platform Terms Actually Mean

What You Retain What You Grant to Facebook
Full Copyright Ownership: You remain the sole legal owner of your original media. A Non-Exclusive License: You give Meta permission to host, transcode, display, and distribute your post globally.
Commercial Rights: You can license, monetize, or sell your video to anyone else. Sub-licensable Rights: Allows Meta's infrastructure partners (CDNs) to stream your video data.
Takedown Rights: You can demand unauthorized copies be removed. Termination upon Deletion: The platform license ends when you delete the post from your account.

What Constitutes Freebotting / Content Theft?

"Freebotting" occurs when a third-party Facebook page downloads your original video and re-uploads it natively to their own page without your permission, often stripping your watermark to farm viral reach and ad revenue. This is illegal copyright infringement. Embedding or sharing your original public post via Facebook's native share button is allowed; ripping and re-uploading the raw video file is not.

How to Protect and Enforce Your Work

  1. Watermark Your Video: Place a subtle, semi-transparent watermark containing your handle near the center of the video canvas.
  2. Enroll in Meta Rights Manager: If you are an active creator, apply for Rights Manager via Meta Business Suite. Rights Manager scans uploaded videos across Facebook and Instagram and automatically flags matching copies.
  3. Submit a DMCA Takedown: If someone steals your work, file an official copyright takedown notice. Platforms are legally required to remove infringing content upon receipt of a valid notice.

The Legal Anatomy of a Copyright Lawsuit

If a commercial business or major media publisher takes your original Facebook video and uses it in a television broadcast or commercial ad without licensing permission, you have substantial legal rights:

  • Cease and Desist Letter: A formal legal warning sent by an intellectual property attorney demanding the removal of the infringing clip and proposing a retroactive licensing fee.
  • Statutory Damages: In the United States, if you registered your copyright with the U.S. Copyright Office prior to the infringement (or within three months of publication), you can seek statutory damages up to $150,000 per willful infringement without needing to prove actual dollar losses.
  • Actual Damages & Profits: Even without early registration, you can recover the actual revenue and advertising profits the infringing company earned using your footage.

Frequently Asked Questions and Practical Advice

Why is direct stream verification critical before local storage?

Direct verification ensures that the downloaded file contains complete video and audio tracks without corrupt moov atoms. On web servers, the moov atom contains the index of frames. If a stream disconnects prematurely, the resulting file will fail to seek or play properly.

How does bandwidth throttling affect mobile video downloads?

Cellular connections often throttle long-lived socket connections to prioritize real-time interactive traffic. To prevent download timeouts, utilize reliable Wi-Fi networks and avoid background app sleep modes when saving large video files.

What are the key ethical principles when handling third-party media?

Always obtain explicit written authorization before re-distributing, editing, or repurposing digital media. Archiving for offline study or personal reference must strictly align with statutory Fair Use principles and platform terms.
