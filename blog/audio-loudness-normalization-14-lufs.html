Audio Loudness Normalization: Mastering to the -14 LUFS Broadcast Standard

Shahrukh Ahmad (SRK AMD) Updated September 29, 2026 8 min read

In the early days of online video, platforms suffered from the "Loudness Wars"—creators would push limiters to their absolute maximum (+2 dB) so their videos sounded louder than their competitors. Today, social media algorithms enforce automated loudness normalization using the international ITU-R BS.1770-4 standard.

Meta targets an integrated loudness of approximately -14 LUFS (Loudness Units Full Scale). If you master your audio too hot (e.g. -8 LUFS), the automated ingest system will aggressively lower your volume, causing pumping and squashed dynamic range.

The Audio Mastering Checklist for Social Video

  1. Integrated Loudness: Target -14 LUFS (+/- 1 LUFS) across the entire duration of the clip.
  2. True Peak Limiting: Set your master limiter ceiling to -1.0 dB True Peak. This leaves sufficient headroom for lossy AAC encoding codecs to avoid inter-sample clipping artifacts.
  3. Dialogue Clarity: Apply a high-pass filter at 80 Hz on vocal tracks to remove air conditioner rumble, followed by gentle multiband compression to keep speech intelligible on mobile phone speakers.
